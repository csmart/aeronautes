# ADR 0095 — delete a prefix or a container by the verified per-name protocol

**Status:** Accepted on 28 October 2026, implemented, and returned to the release on 1 November 2026 by [ADR 0052](0052-v1-capability-gate.md) decision 14; written for the 0.2.x step of the [road to 1.0.0](../audits/2026-10-27/road-to-1.0.0.md), the bulk deletion of a prefix or a container, whose protocol [ADR 0085](0085-reobserved-destructive-identity.md) decided and recorded as owed and which [ADR 0093](0093-selected-set-deletion.md) decision 7 named as the next record.
The bulk deletion runs the verified per-name sweep and the manifest-with-segments deletion is a job that owns the reviewed graph; the two contract rows take the drift decision 9 named, recorded in [the baseline record](../experiments/2026-10-28-bulk-delete-baseline.md).
This record itself returns no capability: the actions stayed under "Bulk delete" with the `bulk-delete` token until the operator's word, which ADR 0052 decision 14 records, and that token no longer exists.
**Scope:** `DeleteService.StartDelete` in its three modes, a prefix, a container's contents and a container, `DeleteService.PreviewDelete`, and `DeleteService.DeleteManifestWithSegments`, whose two branches run the same sweeps.
Benchmark, which writes objects and sweeps them away, follows this record and is not decided here; Mirror is not admitted and is not a step closer.

## The requirement, and what existed when this was written

The bulk actions are built and gated.
`StartDelete` lists the scope page by page and deletes as it streams, either through bulk-delete requests of up to a thousand names, bounded by what the store advertises, or through a pool of eight to sixty-four workers issuing plain DELETEs; it runs up to three passes and stops when a pass issues nothing new; for each invocation it keeps a private SQLite index that counts each failed and unresolved identity once and records every name as issued before its request leaves so no name is requested twice ([ADR 0039](0039-delete-request-authority.md)), an index that [ADR 0036](0036-delete-identity-accounting.md) says is working state and not a durable ledger, bounded to one request's facets per transaction and removed when the invocation closes; it keeps two display samples of two hundred names and writes the failed and unresolved names to retained files through buffered writers; it verifies the scope with a one-row listing after bounded retries (DESIGN §32); the empty mode compares the container's configuration before and after (DESIGN §33); and the delete-container mode deletes the container only after a sweep with no problem.
When that index cannot record an outcome, ADR 0036 stops admitting requests, lets the workers already admitted finish, keeps their attempts as bounded evidence, qualifies the job's counters as incomplete, and never verifies the scope or deletes the container.
`PreviewDelete` counts the objects and bytes under the scope, counts the rows listed as zero bytes or as a static large object as possible manifests, and names the strategy the run would take.
The confirmation is the container name, typed.
`DeleteManifestWithSegments` is a synchronous action, force-only with a typed confirmation and advisory ownership: its request carries the bookmark, the container, the name, the force and the confirmation and nothing of what was reviewed, so it resolves the manifest's graph again at execution, rechecks the manifest's entity tag, deletes the manifest with a plain DELETE, and then, for a static large object, deletes each whole-object entry of that newly resolved list with a plain DELETE of its own, and for a dynamic large object sweeps the manifest's namespace through the prefix sweep after refusing any name observed outside it ([ADR 0044](0044-segment-deletion-namespace.md)); `CheckSegmentOwnership` reports the class, the target as a namespace or a segment count, whether ownership evidence covers the set and how many inline entries the manifest holds, and nothing that identifies the members; the result reports the manifest and the segment counts, and ADR 0036 requires the error to disclose the deleted manifest when the sweep could not account.

What is missing is the comparison.
A plain DELETE deletes whatever stands at the name when it arrives, and a bulk request carries no per-name precondition at all, so a replacement written between the listing and the request is deleted with no record that it was not what the operator approved.
ADR 0085 decided the rules for this path and stated their cost: the baseline for an ordinary row is the listing row, a zero-byte or SLO-listed row takes a HEAD of its own as a later baseline, one DELETE is issued per name immediately after that name's own HEAD, bulk delete is given up, and for N names of which Z take the later baseline the object requests are 2N + Z.
ADR 0093 then built that protocol for a selected set: `Session.DeleteMember` takes a listing row, fixes its baseline, compares under one credentials snapshot, issues the DELETE immediately after the comparison HEAD, reads the name literally afterwards and reports the outcome in the words of [ADR 0092](0092-exact-object-deletion.md) decision 3 and [ADR 0094](0094-marker-and-manifest-deletion-return-ready.md) decision 3; and it made the job item ledger carry a deletion, with members admitted in pages, an issued mark written before the final HEAD, one outcome per member with its baseline and what a read observed, totals the terminal state is derived from, a lost record leaving the job open with its status unavailable, and interrupted members settled on reopen.
The remediation register asks two things of this path: L03, that first-pass and later-pass replacements follow an accepted allowed-effect contract with unchanged collateral, and L04, that the deletion failure streams retain every known and unknown identity beyond the display caps and across a restart.

## Why the selected set's protocol and ledger, run over a listing

The per-name protocol does not care where its row came from.
A prefix sweep's unit is the same listing row the working list carries, so the change to the executor is to hand each row to `DeleteMember` instead of to a plain DELETE or a bulk batch; what is new is the membership, a listing pass rather than a selection, and the volume, which can be millions of names.
A second protocol would have to re-decide every rule ADR 0085 and ADR 0093 settled and would then need its own proof; reusing the one that exists keeps one set of words for what a deletion did.

The same holds for the record of what happened.
The private index of ADR 0036 is not a durable ledger: it lives for one invocation, is removed when it closes, holds only a name and a facet, and its retained files are written through buffers that an abrupt exit need not have flushed, so it cannot say after a restart which names were issued and never settled.
The item ledger already can: it admits members in bounded pages, marks each as issued before its final HEAD, keeps one outcome per member with both identities and what a read observed, derives the terminal state from the members, latches the job's status unavailable when a record is lost, settles interrupted members on reopen, and is read by the drawer in bounded pages of at most two hundred rows by cursor.
Nothing in it is sized to a selection: a page of five hundred members is a page whether the set is a selection or a listing, and no run-wide collection is needed because the sweep holds one listing page at a time, which is the bound K-PERF-001 asks for.
So the sweep's members become durable items in the job's item ledger, the private index and the retained files are retired for the sweeps, and the one cost this adds, two ledger writes per member beside the 2N + Z requests, is measured by the proof before the implementation is accepted rather than assumed either way.

## The behaviour oracle

- ADR 0085: the listing row as the baseline for an ordinary row, a later HEAD for a zero-byte or SLO-listed row, one DELETE per name immediately after its own HEAD, no bulk request, 2N + Z, the worker pool kept, and the interval after the final comparison stated and never closed.
- ADR 0093 decisions 3 and 4: the member protocol with its outcome words, the issued mark before the final HEAD, cancellation between members, a terminal state derived from the members by whether any DELETE was issued, a refused member carrying its kind, and the ledger's fail-closed, status-unavailable and reopen rules.
- ADR 0092 decision 2 and ADR 0094 decision 3: a link in either representation is never deleted, and a manifest is deleted manifest-only by its own protocol and judged by the manifest words.
- ADR 0039: one destructive request per name per job, recorded as issued before it leaves; a pass that issues nothing new ends the sweep; names still listed after the only request are counted, not requested again; a name first listed in a later pass is requested because the prefix scope is the operator's consent; the operator re-runs deliberately for fresh authority.
- ADR 0093 decision 6: a job that never retries a destructive request has no retry scenario, and a changed required set is admitted through the ADR 0069 drift route.
- ADR 0036: a request is never admitted after an accounting failure, workers already admitted finish and their attempts are retained, the scope is not verified and the container is not deleted after such a failure, and the enclosing segment operation discloses a deleted manifest.
- [ADR 0043](0043-cancellation-delta-authority.md): a cancellation produces exactly the accepted delta and a truthful retained outcome.
- [ADR 0020](0020-recovery-containment.md) and ADR 0044: manifest-only by construction, no automatic reclamation, the segment sweep bounded by the manifest's namespace with names outside it refused before any effect, and ownership advisory.
- DESIGN §32 and §33: a bounded streaming pipeline, an operation-specific postcondition stated in the job's effective configuration, cancellation at safe boundaries, exact failed names retained, and the empty mode preserving the container's configuration.
- L03 and L04 of the remediation register, as stated above.

## Decision

1. **Membership is a listing pass, and every object row listed is a member.**
   The sweep lists the scope deeply, page by page, as it does today, and each object row becomes a member row with its name, listed bytes, listed entity tag and static large-object mark, handed to the per-name protocol as the selected set hands its rows.
   An ordinary row is compared against its listing; a row listed as zero bytes or as a static large object takes a HEAD of its own first, which fixes its class and refuses a link at once with its kind; classification comes before the identity comparison; any difference between the baseline and the comparison HEAD refuses the name as changed since listed with both identities recorded; a matching plain object is deleted by one plain DELETE issued immediately after its comparison HEAD; a matching manifest is deleted manifest-only, its segments untouched; a directory marker listed as an object row is a literal object and is deleted as one; and each DELETE is followed by the literal verifying read with the plain or the manifest words.
   No bulk request is sent on any branch, and `BulkDelete` leaves these actions' strategies; the strategy named in the job's effective configuration is the verified per-name one.
2. **Members are durable items, admitted a listing page at a time, and one request attempt per name per job is marked before the final HEAD.**
   Each listing page's object rows are admitted to the job's item ledger, with their listed facts, before any name of that page is issued, in the ledger's own pages of at most five hundred; a page the ledger cannot record ends the sweep before any of its names is requested, as a selected set's admission does.
   A later pass admits only the names the ledger does not yet hold, asked of the ledger a page at a time, so no run-wide set of names is kept in memory.
   ADR 0039 decision 1 is kept and moved to where the protocol needs it: a member's issued mark is written before its final HEAD, not before a batch, and a name marked issued is never requested again by the same job in any later pass.
   That covers a name refused or found changed since listed as much as one deleted: the job does not try such a name again with a fresher row, because the comparison that refused it is the protocol's answer for this job, and the operator re-runs the deletion deliberately to obtain fresh authority for whatever is still listed, as ADR 0039 decision 4 records.
   A later pass therefore requests only the names first listed in that pass, each against its own listed facts, because the prefix scope is the operator's consent; a pass that admits nothing new ends the sweep, and a pass that lists nothing ends it as before.
   This is L03's allowed-effect contract for this path: a replacement at an issued name is never requested by the job that issued it, and a name first listed in a later pass is deleted only if it still matches what that pass listed.
3. **Workers run members concurrently, each member whole, and the adjacency is per worker.**
   The bounded worker pool stays, as ADR 0085 kept it, and each worker runs one member's issued mark, baseline, comparison, DELETE and verifying read as one unit under one credentials snapshot, exactly as the selected set runs one member: that worker issues no request of its own between the member's comparison HEAD and its DELETE, and no authentication on that worker's credentials falls between them.
   Other workers' comparison HEADs, DELETEs, verifying reads, authentications and ledger writes may interleave with that pair on the wire; the protocol's guarantee is per member, and the interval it leaves open after a member's final HEAD is the one ADR 0085 states for every path, not widened by the pool.
   Cancellation lands between members on every worker: a member in flight finishes its own requests and records its outcome, no later member starts, and the listing stops being consumed, so the delta is exact per name (ADR 0043) and the boundary is the one DESIGN §32.1 asks for.
4. **Every outcome is one durable record per member, and a lost record stops admission without interrupting a member.**
   Each member's outcome is recorded when its verifying read settles, with the baseline its comparison was made against and what a read observed at the name, in the words ADR 0093 decision 4 gives the ledger: deleted, with the successor when something stood at the name; unverified, with what the read observed; changed since listed, with the identity expected and the one found; refused, with its kind; failed at the request, with the store's status; and not attempted for a member the job never issued.
   The job carries exact counts derived from the ledger, planned, issued, deleted, unverified, changed, refused, failed and issued-without-outcome, and the drawer shows every member's record in pages, so no identity is lost to a display cap and the records survive a restart with the job, under the operator's job retention setting (L04).
   When an issued mark or an outcome cannot be recorded, no further member is issued on any worker; a member already past its issued mark finishes whole, because a member in flight is never interrupted inside its own requests, and its outcome is recorded if the ledger still allows it, otherwise it stays issued with no outcome, which the totals count as unsettled and the reopen rule settles as unresolved; the job's status becomes unavailable with the retained reason, new jobs are blocked for the session, the scope is not verified and the container is never deleted.
   This is ADR 0036's rule in the ledger's terms: admission stops, admitted workers join, and what they may have done is retained as the issued marks that outlive the process.
5. **The terminal state follows the members and the postcondition.**
   A sweep is complete only when every member it issued was deleted and the one-row listing of the scope is empty after bounded retries; partial whenever a DELETE was issued and the job is not complete, which covers a member refused or changed beside the deleted ones, every reply lost, a cancellation after a DELETE, names still listed after the job's only request for them, and recording that became unavailable after a DELETE; cancelled when cancellation landed before any DELETE was issued; and failed when nothing was sent, because every listed member was refused or changed since listed, because the listing or the ledger could not be read before any request, or because the postcondition found the scope not empty when this job had listed nothing to request, as happens when a writer creates a name between an empty listing and the verification.
   A failed job of that last kind says that it sent no request and that the scope held a name it never listed, keeps the sampled row count as its evidence, and never takes the delete-container step; the operator re-runs for fresh authority.
   The postcondition and the pass count stay in the job's effective configuration, so the verification is never a silent shortcut.
6. **The empty and delete-container modes keep their contracts.**
   Emptying a container compares its configuration before and after and reports a difference as a problem, unchanged.
   Deleting a container issues the container DELETE only after a complete sweep with an empty postcondition; a partial, cancelled, failed or unavailable sweep never deletes the container, which is then reported with what the sweep left.
7. **The preview states the cost, and the confirmation says what the protocol does.**
   `PreviewDelete` keeps its counts and adds the request cost: N objects, of which Z are listed as zero bytes or as a static large object and take a later baseline, cost 2N + Z object requests before listing, retries or verification; the strategy it names is the verified per-name one.
   The dialog states the scope, the counts, the cost, the container's versioning consequence in the sentence ADR 0092 settled, that an object listed with a size is deleted only if it still matches its listing and one that changed is left and recorded, that a zero-byte or SLO-listed row is read again first and what that read finds is what is deleted, a marker or a manifest included and a link refused, that each name is requested once by this job, and that a manifest's segments stay; the confirmation is still the container name, typed (DESIGN §28.2).
8. **Deleting a manifest with its segments becomes a job that owns the reviewed graph, the manifest and the segments.**
   `DeleteManifestWithSegments` keeps its force, its typed confirmation and its advisory ownership, and becomes a job of a new type whose members are durable items in the same ledger, because a synchronous call owns nothing once it has deleted the manifest and cannot say afterwards what it did to the segments.
   The graph the operator reviews is the graph the job acts on: the review returns, beside what it reports today, the reviewed membership, for a static large object the exact whole-object entries as resolved, each with its container, object, size and entity tag as the manifest records them, and for a dynamic large object the canonical namespace, together with the manifest's identity as reviewed; the request carries that membership back, and the job admits it to the ledger before any effect, the manifest as the first member and each entry as a member keyed by container and object, a reference repeated in the manifest admitted once, so the reviewed set survives a crash at any point and is never a graph resolved again at execution.
   The manifest's content identity binds its class, logical length and entity tag and not its graph, and an equal-tag graph swap keeps that tuple, so the graph is compared too: for a static large object the job reads the manifest's entries immediately before the manifest member's comparison HEAD, under the same credentials snapshot, and refuses the job when they differ from the reviewed membership; for a dynamic large object the namespace the comparison HEAD itself reports is compared with the reviewed one, and the refusal of names observed outside the namespace is made before the manifest is touched (ADR 0044).
   A static graph swapped with equal content between that read and the comparison HEAD is the exposure this leaves: the manifest is deleted, because its identity matched, and no segment outside the reviewed set is ever touched, because the members are the reviewed ones; nothing here widens the set a swap could reach.
   The manifest member is deleted by the manifest protocol of ADR 0094 decision 3, compared against the identity reviewed under one credentials snapshot, manifest-only, with the literal verifying read, instead of by a plain DELETE after an entity-tag recheck.
   The segment phase begins only after the manifest member's outcome is durably recorded as deleted: a manifest refused or changed since it was reviewed ends the job failed with nothing deleted; a manifest whose DELETE the store refused, or whose outcome is unverified because its reply was lost, its literal read failed or a manifest of the same kind still stands at the name, ends the job partial with that outcome recorded and every segment untouched, because an unconfirmed manifest deletion is not consent to remove what it may still reference.
   The segment members then follow the per-name protocol: for a static large object each reviewed entry with its recorded size and entity tag as the listed facts, so a segment is compared against what the manifest said it was, and an entry without both facts takes a later baseline as a zero-byte row does; for a dynamic large object the namespace's listing rows through this sweep, admitted a page at a time after the manifest.
   The job's record is the durable owner of both effects, and two lost-record boundaries are distinct: when the manifest member's own outcome cannot be recorded after its DELETE, only its issued mark survives, the member is unresolved on reopen, the job's status is unavailable and no segment is attempted, and neither the job message nor recovery may turn that issued mark into a deleted outcome; when a later write fails after the manifest's deleted outcome committed, that record stands, the segment members follow decision 4, and the job's status is unavailable with the deleted manifest disclosed by the record and the terminal message, which is how the disclosure ADR 0036 requires is given.
   A job interrupted by a shutdown is settled on reopen as any deletion job is, with the reviewed members it had admitted; the result returned to the caller is the job's id, the drawer shows the manifest and each segment with its outcome, and nothing here reclaims a segment on its own authority (ADR 0020).
9. **The gate and the contract.**
   The actions stay deferred under "Bulk delete" and return together on the operator's word.
   `DeleteService.StartDelete` is a job row whose required set is today the job default, happy, refusal, conflict, cancel, partial and retry, and whose admitted baseline protects its credited happy, refusal and partial cells; it becomes an explicit job row requiring happy, refusal, conflict, cancel and partial, with retry dropped because this job never retries a destructive request, as ADR 0093 decision 6 decided for the selected set, and with the three protected cells kept credited by their producers while conflict is credited for the first time.
   `DeleteService.DeleteManifestWithSegments` keeps its name and is today a destructive row requiring happy, refusal, conflict, partial, retry and cancel, with its refusal cell protected; it becomes a job row requiring happy, refusal, conflict, cancel and partial, with retry dropped for the same reason, and the refusal before the force stays credited by its producer under the job row.
   The expected drift at the candidate run is exactly those two rows, the retry scenario leaving both and the kind of the second changing, with no protected pair missing; the changed required sets are admitted through the [ADR 0069](0069-preserve-covered-scenario-cells.md) drift route from that run, as the selected set's row was.
10. **What follows from it.**
    Benchmark's cleanup becomes a verified sweep of the names it wrote, under its own record.
    Mirror stays without a token and is not admitted by anything here, because a name absent from the source has no identity to compare against (ADR 0085).

## What this does not touch

Exact-object, selected-set, marker and manifest deletion keep their records; copy and upload are untouched; automatic segment reclamation stays refused; the valuable-data release hold stays in force.
Nothing here is a lock, a reservation or exclusive ownership, and a member deleted after a match may still have been replaced by a writer landing after its final HEAD, as ADR 0085 states for every path.

## Required proof before implementation is accepted

Tier 1 under DESIGN §65.12.
Backend cases run through the public service on the mock and the owned Swift, with namespace snapshots before and after.

- Happy: a prefix holding plain objects, a zero-byte object, a marker, a static and a dynamic manifest and nested prefixes is deleted; every member's record says deleted; the manifests' segments, every unselected neighbour and the other account are unchanged; the job is complete with an empty postcondition; the request log shows one HEAD-then-DELETE pair and one literal read per member, a baseline HEAD only for the zero-byte and SLO-listed rows, no bulk request and no DELETE repeated.
- Refusal: a link under the prefix is refused with its kind, the rest deleted, the job partial, and neither the link nor its target touched.
- Conflict: an ordinary row replaced after the listing, by a plain object, a manifest and a link in turn, is refused as changed since listed with both identities retained, nothing is deleted for it, the rest are deleted, and the job is partial.
- Later pass: a name written after the first pass's listing is deleted in a later pass against its own row; a name refused in the first pass is not requested again in any later pass although it is still listed; a pass that admits nothing new ends the sweep; names still listed after the sweep's only request are counted and the job is partial.
- Cancel: a cancellation during a long sweep ends the job with the members before it deleted, the one in flight on each worker settled, nothing later issued and the counts exact; a cancellation before any DELETE ends it cancelled with nothing deleted.
- Partial through verification: a member whose DELETE reply is lost is unverified, the job partial, and no DELETE is repeated.
- No request and a non-empty scope: an empty first listing followed by a writer creating a name before the one-row verification ends the job failed, with no request sent, the sampled row retained as evidence and the container not deleted.
- Modes: emptying preserves and compares the container's configuration; deleting a container refuses the container DELETE after a partial, failed or unavailable sweep and issues it after a complete one.
- Scale and retention: a sweep of more members than a ledger page and than the drawer's page, on the mock, with its resident memory bounded to a listing page and the ledger's write cost per member measured and recorded; every member's record readable in the drawer's pages and after the process restarts; the counts on the job exact.
- Recording failures: an issued mark that cannot be written stops the sweep before that name's HEAD; an outcome that cannot be recorded after a DELETE stops further issuing while a worker held at its own comparison HEAD still issues its DELETE and verifying read and nothing else, with its outcome recorded or its issued mark retained, the job's status unavailable, no postcondition run and no container deleted.
- Adjacency: no request of the same worker's and no authentication on that worker's credentials runs between any member's comparison HEAD and its DELETE, under a gate that places a manifest at the name during any such request; a deliberate interleaving, one worker held between its HEAD and DELETE while another completes a member, shows the held worker's DELETE still following its own HEAD with nothing of its own between, and the exposure after that HEAD stated.
- Preview and dialog: the counts, the cost 2N + Z and the strategy; the dialog's sentences and the typed container name; the segment dialog's membership and boundary.
- Segment job, the graph: the reviewed membership carried by the request and admitted before any effect; a static manifest whose graph was swapped for one of equal content between the review and the execution refused with nothing deleted; a dynamic manifest whose namespace moved refused likewise; two reviewed entries with the same object name in different containers each deleted once; a reference repeated in the manifest deleted once; the job reopened after a crash immediately following the manifest's deletion settling every admitted member.
- Segment job, the manifest: the manifest deleted by the manifest protocol and refused when changed; a manifest refused, changed, failed at its DELETE or unverified leaving every segment untouched with the job failed or partial as decision 8 says; a static member compared against the manifest's entries with a changed segment refused; the dynamic branch bounded by the namespace with the outside-name refusal before any effect; a manifest deleted and no segment attempted, through a lost listing and through every segment refused, ending partial with the manifest's outcome recorded.
- Segment job, lost records: the manifest member's own outcome write failing after its DELETE, leaving its issued mark only, no segment attempted, the job unavailable and the member unresolved on reopen with no deleted outcome anywhere; a later write failing after the manifest's deleted outcome committed, leaving that record standing and the job unavailable.
- Retained mutants, each failing a case above: a bulk request used; the listed row ignored in favour of a HEAD baseline for an ordinary row; the replacement adopted as the baseline when an ordinary row's comparison finds a manifest; a DELETE repeated after a lost reply; cancellation honoured inside a member; the issued mark written after the HEAD; a refused name requested again in a later pass; a new member issued after a lost record; the container deleted after a partial sweep; a non-empty postcondition with no request reported complete; a conflict recorded without its identities; the preview cost counting N alone; the manifest deleted by a plain DELETE without the comparison; the graph resolved again at execution instead of the reviewed membership; the segment phase started after an unverified manifest; a deleted outcome fabricated from the manifest's issued mark.
- The candidate run's drift diagnostic retained and the replacement baseline built from its reports.

## What this does not establish

It does not bound the interval after a member's final HEAD, prove that bytes are gone, or identify an observed successor's origin.
A listing is advisory: a name it omits is neither deleted nor reported, and the postcondition's one row is evidence that the scope is empty, not a scan.
It does not lift the gate, decide Benchmark or Mirror, or close the valuable-data release hold.
